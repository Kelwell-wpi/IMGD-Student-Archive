The main focus of my work was investigating various methods of outline shaders, particularly for use with characters with complex silhouettes and animations. Testing was done with a character model that also had clothing assets and other accessories. The two approaches taken were a material based shader and a post process shader, each of which having their fair share of complications. All shader work was done in UE 5.6.1.

Encountered Issues

Faceting/World Tangent Issues

Faceting of the outline and the appearance of the model when only viewing in world tangent mode. As shown, softened normals should have a smooth gradient of color across the mesh, rather than several gradients along individual polygons. Below is how to access the world tangent visualization.

One major issue was the seemingly random faceting of the outline. Fortunately, this was discovered to be caused by improper world tangents, and is an issue with the baking process of the world tangent map. This would likely be fixed by either troubleshooting the baking in Substance Painter or by ensuring that the model used for baking had softened normals.

Due to the shader’s reliance on softened normals, any objects with sharp corners will have to be modified to avoid gapping at corners.

Gapping/Fresnel Issues

Significant gapping issues, especially on rounded surfaces when viewed head-on.

Likely due to the natural limitations of this shader being fresnel based and not rotating produced geometry to face the camera, it causes these gaps. This forum thread and related video may be a jumping-off point for a solution, but I did not find it to be a proper solution in my implementation.

Encountered Issues (Post Process)

Static Line Weight

The nondynamic line weight meant that models had unusually thick line weights at a distance and unusually thin line weights up close.

Despite the shader having a certain distance threshold where it would lock the thickness of the outline, the outline weight felt very static.

Ghosting

Animation with material based (top) vs. screen based (bottom). The screen based shader has a distinct unsteadiness in the line weight (especially when looking at the side of the character’s face) as it is seen getting wider as the head pulls up, and thinner as the head pulls down. Both clips are using TSR AA.

More prominent than on the material based outline, the screen based outline has significant ghosting (especially with thicker line thicknesses). This ghosting is worst with TSR (Temporal Super Resolution) anti aliasing, but TSR offers the cleanest edges.
